ABOUT

Ebbfall is a free real-time strategy game for the browser, on a phone or a desktop, made for fun. In 2047, time travel was discovered. The only open gate led six centuries back. Naturally, we used it to build data centers. The local king had other plans. The Expedition brought bots, buggies, drones and a satellite, hired the locals, and dug in. The Kingdom has castles, archers, knights and a very long memory. Play against one to seven computer players at three levels, or with friends online: open a room and send its link, with no account and nothing to install. Once opened it plays offline too, and installs as an app.

Made by Stelian Matei. Everything is generated by code: the 3D-rendered sprites, the painted terrain, the synthesized sounds and voices. The game is a deterministic simulation in Rust, compiled to WebAssembly.

How to play

Mine gold, raise Cottages for population (the Expedition: wind turbines for Power, and Recruiting posts for hired places), which every unit needs (up to 90, 100 or 120 by the map's size; the Expedition's Power and hired places each have their own share of it, 68 and 22 on the smallest maps), raise an army, and bring down the enemy's base before their waves bring down yours: a side is out when its last building falls, or when it has nothing left to fight with and no gold to train more. The enemy base is on the far side of the map.

  • Mouse: left-click or drag to select, right-click to move, attack, gather or set a rally point; hold the middle button and drag to move the map. In the panel, click a unit for it alone, Shift+click to drop it. Double-click selects all of that unit type on screen (on a factory: all of them, and they train as one).
  • Keyboard, in one of two schemes (change them in OPTIONS, on the menu's first page or the pause menu). Classic: the arrow keys or the screen's edge move the map (WASD too while nothing is selected), and each button's letter is its key: A attack-move, S stop, H hold. WASD: WASD always move the map, and a button's key is its place on the card: ERT the top row, FGH the middle one, CVB the bottom one (each button shows its key). In both: Shift with a unit's key trains five, Ctrl+1–9 saves a control group, , idle factory, Q whole army (twice: look at it), Space jump to the last attack (again: the ones before), Home back to base, Esc cancel or let go, P pause.
  • Touch: tap to select; tap the ground to move, tap an enemy to attack, tap a gold mine to gather. Drag the map with one finger to look around (flick to glide), pinch to zoom. Hold SELECT (or tap it) and drag to select a group; double-tap a unit for all of its type, or a factory for all of them (they train as one, the shortest queue first). Drag on the minimap to nudge the view, tap it to jump; HOME beside SELECT brings you home, and ARMY after it selects your whole army (tap it again to look at them). With a building selected, a tap on the ground lets go of it; its rally point is set with Rally. workers given a job (gather, build, repair) are let go, so your next tap does not pull them off it; after placing a building the Build list reopens for the next one. Tap a gold mine (with nothing selected) to see how much gold is left; a worker left idle for a while pings the minimap. With a group selected, tap a type in the panel at the bottom to keep only those, hold it to drop them. Tap a queued unit (in the panel) to cancel it; hold any command button to see what it does and costs.
  • Select a worker (a Villager or a Bot) and tap Build to place buildings. What needs another building first is under Advanced, which opens once your Barracks (the Expedition: Recruiting post) stands; each building shows up as it opens, after the ones already there. The Kingdom's Market earns about a gold every three seconds by itself, with no workers, for as long as it stands (one per Castle). Workers mine only when you send them: a new one waits by your Castle (the Expedition: Data center), or walks to a mine its rally point is set on and waits there, and a worker who finishes a building waits beside it; select them and tap the mine (or right-click it). Up to eight Villagers (or five Bots, who carry more) mine a gold mine at once, each going in and coming out with a load of gold; more wait their turn at its side, and a worker at a crowded mine moves to a quieter one nearby. Villagers also repair damaged buildings (the Repair button, or right-click the building); repairs cost a quarter of the price for a full fix. A Bot stays at each building until it stands, and the Expedition does not repair.
  • The Kingdom: the Barracks trains Guards and Archers, the Stables Riders and Knights (once a Market stands), the Workshop Ballistas, Cannons and the Balloon; the Blacksmith unlocks Knights, Cannons and Ballistas. Guards are cheap spearmen; once Shield Drill is researched they can Brace: more armor, but no moving. An Archer's arrow slows what it hits, flyers too: half speed and no ability for 3 s. A Cannon sets up by itself whenever it stops, and packs up when it has to move (3 s each way). Set up, it outranges everything on the ground and its cannonballs splash, but it cannot hit flyers nor anything close to it, and it fires only at what your side can see: keep an escort with it, and a spotter out front. The Ballista is the answer to flyers.
  • The Expedition: the Recruiting post hires Sellswords, and Bruisers once a Lab stands (build as many posts as you like: each gives hired places, up to the map's maximum); the Factory builds Buggies, Rovers (once a Lab stands) and Drones; the Portal trains Androids. Sellswords (light swords) are the line, Bruisers (big shields) the toughest on foot; Rovers fire rockets on the move; Drones fly fast and raid workers; Androids are its elite, slow to make and costly, tough and long-ranged, and shoot ground and air. Its machines (Bots, Buggies, Rovers, Drones and Androids) run on Power, and faster near a Data center or an Uplink. The Data center launches a satellite once a Lab stands; then the Uplink, then the Portal: one per player, and it uses Power while it stands.
